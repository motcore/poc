Motcore v6 — Apex Pivot

Vertical cross-section of one cube axis. The output cone does not translate: it pivots about ±2° around the vertex it shares with the two motor cones. That vertex falls inside the motor shaft, so no bearing can sit there — the rotation comes from a four-bar linkage whose link axes converge on exactly that point.

What you are looking at

The dashed purple lines are the axes of the two links, extended. They cross at the purple ring: that is where the carriage is actually turning at that instant, even though nothing physical sits there. The cross marks the vertex shared by all three cones, and the green dot is the carriage's own vertex.

Notice that the green dot barely leaves the cross — 0.22 mm over the whole stroke. That is the error that matters, because it is the one that displaces the real vertex and puts sliding into the contact. The purple ring, on the other hand, does wander once you leave the centre: the instant centre of a four-bar always travels, and only coincides with the vertex in the neutral position. Put another way, the mechanism does not turn exactly about the vertex — it turns almost about the vertex, and that "almost" is two tenths of a millimetre.

The servo does not push the carriage through a slider but through a telescopic link with two springs inside, pinned at both ends. At first the carriage follows it with almost no load. The moment the rubber layers touch, the carriage stops and every remaining degree of servo travel goes into compressing the spring: preload is set by force, not by position. And because the crank works near its dead centre during that phase, the servo pushes hard while barely loading its gearbox.
